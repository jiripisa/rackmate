Getting started

Rackmate hosts Audio Units so that a Mac can stand in a dawless studio as one more instrument, without opening a DAW. It needs macOS 15 or later on Apple silicon. It hosts Audio Units only, and the plugins are the ones already on your Mac.

  1. Download the disk image from the download page and open it.
  2. Drag Rackmate to Applications and start it from there. The Start screen opens.
  3. Choose New rack. If your audio device has inputs, macOS asks once for the microphone; answer it. See Microphone permission.
  4. The Add dialog is open with the search field ready. Type a few letters of a plugin's name and press Return. The plugin appears as channel 1 of the rack.
  5. On the channel, click No MIDI under MIDI in and pick your MIDI device, then check the Audio out display. On a device with four or more outputs the first instrument starts on 3-4, not on 1-2.
  6. Press ⌘S and give the rack a name. Next time it is on the Start screen.

If you used Rackmate under its old name, Pluginarium, your racks, presets and settings come along at the first launch. See Where racks are stored.

Start screen

The app always opens here, on the Rackmate logo and your racks. No audio device is touched until you open a rack.

1Ready
The app is up.
2Saved racks
One row per rack, by name, with the plugin of each channel under the name. Click a row to open the rack. With nothing saved yet the list reads No racks yet. The list scrolls when it no longer fits.
3New rack
Opens an empty rack and the Add dialog over it.
4Settings
Opens Settings. Done brings you back here.

The rack

The main screen. Channels stand one above the other like units in a studio rack, and in each unit the signal runs from left to right. When there are more channels than the window shows, the units scroll up and down and the top bar stays. Under the last unit a vent panel fills any free height; it does nothing. The window opens at 1280 by 760 points and goes down to 1200 by 600.

1Top bar
The rack and its save state, the MIDI clock, the CPU load and Settings.
2Channels
One unit per instrument or standalone effect, numbered from the top.
3Add instrument or effect
Opens the Add dialog for a new channel.

A rack plays as soon as it is open. There is no play button: the plugins make sound when they get MIDI or audio.

Top bar

1Rack name ▾
Opens the rack menu, below. A rack without a name yet reads New rack.
2Save state
Saved or Unsaved changes, with a Save button when there is something to save. See Saving. A rack that was never saved and has no channels shows nothing here.
3MIDI clock
No MIDI clock with an unlit lamp, or the source and its tempo, such as Hapax clock, 120.0 BPM, with the lamp blinking on the beat. See MIDI clock.
4CPU load
How much of the audio time the app uses, such as CPU 17 %.
5Gear
The key with the tooltip Settings. Opens Settings. Done brings you back to the rack, and the rack plays on meanwhile.

Audio messages appear in the same bar, left of the MIDI clock: Audio device missing, Audio could not start, Waiting for microphone access and Microphone access denied, inputs are silent. See Faults.

Rack menu

Load rack
Your racks by name, the current one in amber. Choosing one replaces what plays. Unsaved changes are asked about first.
New rack
Starts an empty rack and opens the Add dialog.
Save as
Saves a copy under a new name, which starts as the old name plus copy. From then on you work in the copy, and the original stays as it was saved.

A channel

A channel is one rack unit, with one instrument plugin or one standalone effect in it. It grows with what it holds, and it shows only what its plugin can use, so a normal synth has no audio input displays. The plate of a standalone effect's channel has a slightly different colour.

1Number and ×
The channel's place in the rack, and the button that removes it. The app asks Remove channel? first.
2Activity lamp
On an instrument it flashes green when MIDI comes in, on a standalone effect when audio comes out. Its tooltip says which: Lights when MIDI comes in or Lights when audio comes out.
3MIDI in display
The MIDI channel the plugin listens to. Audio in and Sidechain in displays appear in this column too, for a plugin that has them.
4MIDI device
Where the plugin's notes come from. Click for the device menu.
5Maker, picture and plugin name
Keys for an instrument, a wave for a standalone effect, pads for a plugin tagged as a sampler. A long name shrinks to fit and is then cut off. The tooltip shows all of it. The plugin's maker sits above, as the plugin reports it; a plugin that is not installed shows none.
6Preset display
The current sound, in amber. Click for the preset menu.
7Open plugin key
Opens the plugin's own window.
8Follow Start/Stop key
Whether the plugin starts and stops with the MIDI clock.
9Effects
The effects behind the plugin, from left to right.
10Audio out display
The output channels of the audio device.
11Level meters
Segmented bars for left and right, spanning 60 dB, showing what leaves the channel. They are green, then yellow from 68 % of the bar and red from 88 %.

The two square keys have pictures and no text. Hold the pointer over a key for its name. A key that is on lights green and glows. Amber is for displays, green for what is lit, and red for faults.

Displays

Every route on a channel is a value on a display, and clicking the value opens its menu. The digits are seven-segment digits, and unlit segments stay faintly visible, as on hardware. When the pointer is over a display that can open, its border turns amber.

In these menus the current value is in amber. A click anywhere else closes the menu.

MIDI in

Shown for plugins that take MIDI. The display reads 1 to 16, or ALL for every channel. With no device it shows a dimmed --- and does not open. Click it to pick All or a channel from 1 to 16.

Under the display stands the device, in small text. Click it to pick No MIDI or one of the MIDI sources macOS reports, under their system names, virtual ports included. A new device keeps the channel filter, and a channel that had no device starts on All.

A source that is gone stays listed as Hapax (not connected), and the device itself reads Hapax is not connected in red. A device behind a MIDI router can stay listed while it is switched off, because macOS still reports the router's port.

Audio in

Shown for a plugin with a main audio input, such as a vocoder, and always for a standalone effect. The display reads a stereo pair such as 3-4 or 11-12, a mono channel such as 3, or a dimmed OFF. The menu lists each input of the audio device as a mono channel, such as In 1, then the stereo pairs, such as In 1–2, in two columns.

On an instrument it starts at Off, so a synth with an input never gets signal by accident, and a vocoder stays without its microphone until you pick one. A standalone effect has no Off, because it has no other source. A plugin that takes its modulator through a sidechain shows Sidechain in instead.

Sidechain in

Shown for a plugin with a sidechain input. It works like Audio in and lists the same inputs, with Off first. The source is always an input of the audio device, not another channel. An effect in the chain sets its sidechain in its three-dot menu.

Audio out

The display reads the route the same way, such as 3-4. The menu lists the stereo pairs of the audio device, such as Out 3–4, then each mono channel, such as Out 3, in two columns. Several channels can share an output; they add up. A route the device does not have stays listed, the display lights red, and the channel says so under it: Out 5–6 is not available on this device.

Open plugin

The first key opens the plugin's own window above the rack and lights green. Its tooltip changes from Open plugin to Close plugin, and pressing it again closes the window; the window's red button does the same. Each plugin has at most one window, sized as the plugin asks. The key is dimmed while the plugin cannot open, such as when it is still loading or is missing.

Plugin windows step aside for dialogs and Settings and come back where they were. A plugin that shows no view gets a small window that reads This plugin has no window. An effect opens its window when you click its name.

Follow Start/Stop

The second key decides what the plugin sees of the MIDI clock's transport. Its tooltip reads Follow Start/Stop: On or Off. Tempo reaches every plugin either way.

Key lit, on
The plugin sees tempo and transport, so an arpeggiator or internal sequencer runs with your sequencer.
Key dark, off
The plugin sees the tempo only, and to it the transport is always stopped. A delay keeps its tempo, and an internal sequencer stays still.

It starts off on instruments, so a sequencer never starts a part you did not ask for, and on for effects, both standalone and in a chain. The key is saved in the rack. Notes and controllers follow MIDI in whatever the key says.

Presets

A preset is a named snapshot of a plugin's sound. It belongs to the plugin, so it is there in every rack that uses that plugin. The preset display of a channel shows the current sound:

Default sound
The plugin as it loads, untouched. It can only be chosen when adding a plugin.
A name
One of your presets, or a factory preset.
Custom sound
You changed the sound in the plugin's window, with no preset picked.
A name with (changed)
You changed the sound after picking one of your presets. Factory presets never get this mark.

Plugins do not report that their sound changed. Rackmate compares the plugin's state when its window opens and when it closes, so a change shows when you close the window or save.

My presets
The presets you saved for this plugin, or None saved yet. The current one is in amber.
Factory presets
The sounds the plugin lists as an Audio Unit.
Save as preset
Asks for a name and stores the sound the plugin has now. The same name for the same plugin replaces the old preset. The field starts from the name the sound has.

Why some plugins show no factory list

A plugin decides whether its factory sounds are visible to a host. Mariana lists them; TB-303 and JUPITER-8 keep them inside their own window. For such a plugin the menu says This plugin keeps its factory sounds in its own window. Open it, choose a sound and save it here. Do that once, and the sound is one of your presets from then on.

What happens to a saved preset

A preset is stored for good the moment you save it, even if you then choose Don't save for the rack. What waits for the rack is only the fact that it uses that preset. See Saving. A rack holds each plugin's full state, so changing a preset later does not change a rack saved earlier.

Effects

Effects in the chain of a channel process that channel's plugin. They get their signal from the chain, so the only input they can have is a sidechain. The chain runs from left to right and scrolls sideways when it does not fit.

1Parallel
Effects one above the other in a stage run in parallel, joined by brackets. Up to three share a stage. The branches are added at equal level; set the balance with each plugin's own wet and dry controls.
2Series
Stages run from left to right, joined by arrows, each one feeding the next.
3Plus
Adds an effect. See Add effect.
1Picture
Hidden when the slot is narrower than 150 points.
2Name
In amber. Click it to open or close the effect's own window. While the window is open the name turns white. Drag it to move the effect.
3Lamp
The on and off switch. Green is on.
4Three dots
Opens the three-dot menu. The tooltip reads Menu.

On and off

Click the lamp of an effect to turn it off or on, or choose Turn effect off or Turn effect on in its menu. The state is saved with the rack, and the change marks the rack unsaved. An effect that is off has a dark lamp and a grey name.

An effect that is off stays loaded and keeps its window, presets and state. It leaves the signal path:

  • In series, the signal passes it unchanged.
  • In a parallel stage, it drops out of the sum, so the level does not jump.
  • When all effects of a stage are off, the stage passes the signal dry.

Turning an effect off cuts its tail, and what its buffers held may sound when it returns.

Add effect

The round plus at the end of a chain unfolds into a labelled Add effect button when the pointer is over it or it has the keyboard focus, and folds back afterwards. With Reduce Motion on in macOS it changes without moving. It opens the Add dialog and puts the effect in a new stage at the end of the chain.

Three-dot menu

Open plugin / Close plugin
The effect's own window, the same as clicking its name.
Turn effect off / Turn effect on
The same as clicking its lamp.
Presets: Default sound
Names the current sound and opens the preset menu.
Sidechain in: Off
Only for a plugin with a sidechain. Pick an input of the audio device. The slot then reads, for example, Sidechain from In 1.
Follow Start/Stop: On
Switches Follow Start/Stop for this effect.
Add parallel effect
Opens the Add dialog and puts the effect in this stage. Not offered once three share the stage.
Move to series
Only in a parallel stage. Moves the effect to a new stage right after this one.
Remove
Removes the effect at once, without asking.

Dragging

Drag an effect by its name to move it. The drag starts after 5 points. The effect dims in its place, a ghost follows the pointer, and an amber line shows where it will land.

Outer 30 % of a slot's width
A new series stage before or after that slot's stage.
Middle of a slot
Parallel, in that slot's stage: above the slot from its upper half, below it from its lower half. A stage with three effects offers only the sides.
Anywhere else in a channel's effects area
The end of that channel's chain, also when the chain is empty.
On the effect itself, or outside the effects areas
Nothing changes.

A drag can end in another channel. The plugin is not reloaded and keeps its sound, and its open window closes first. The move marks the rack unsaved. An effect that failed to load does not drag.

To reach a channel out of view, hold the drag within 40 points of the rack's top or bottom edge. The rack scrolls toward that edge at a steady pace, and stops at its end or when the pointer leaves the edge.

Add dialog

The same dialog adds a channel, adds an effect and replaces a missing plugin. A new rack opens it right away, with the search field ready, so a few letters and Return are enough.

1Instrument and Standalone effect
Only when adding a channel. Instruments are Audio Units of type aumu and aumf, effects are aufx.
2Search plugins
Filters by plugin name and vendor as you type. With nothing left it reads No plugin matches this search.
3Plugin list
Recent holds the last five plugins you added, then come All the others. Without recent plugins the list is headed Instruments or Effects.
4Sound
Default sound, then My presets and Factory presets of the selected plugin. A plugin without a factory list shows Factory sounds stay inside this plugin. Choose one in its window and save it as a preset.
5Routing
The fields the plugin has: MIDI device, Channel, Audio in, Sidechain in and Audio out. They start from the position tables, and you can change them here or later on the channel. Only when adding a channel.
6Cancel and Add
The second button reads Replace when replacing a plugin.

The first time you select a plugin, the app loads it once to learn its inputs and presets, and remembers the answer. That can stop the screen for a moment. Audio on the other channels keeps running.

The three uses

Add to rack
A new channel, from Add instrument or effect or a new rack.
Add effect to TB-303
An effect in a chain, from the plus or from Add parallel effect. No tabs and no routing.
Replace plugin
A new plugin for a channel whose plugin is missing. The channel keeps its routing and effects. It keeps its audio in if the new plugin has one, and its sidechain in likewise.

Standalone effects

A standalone effect is a channel of its own. It takes signal from an input of the audio device and sends it to an output, so any hardware instrument wired to your interface can reach it. Add one from the Standalone effect tab. It has no plugin in front of it, and it can have effects of its own.

A standalone effect passes its input to its output as soon as it is added. If that output reaches the input again, for example through a microphone near your monitors, it can feed back. Keep the monitors low until the routing is right.

Saving

A rack holds its channels, routing, effects and the full state of every plugin. Any change to it marks it Unsaved changes, and Save or ⌘S saves it. Save and Save as are also in the File menu. Changes in Settings are not part of a rack and are stored at once.

While a plugin window is open, the top bar shows Save even next to Saved, because that window may hold a change the app has not heard of. Saving then looks at the open windows first, and does nothing if no sound changed.

After a save a line at the bottom of the window says so, such as Evening set saved.

The questions

Save changes?
Comes when you load another rack, start a new one or quit with unsaved changes. Save saves and goes on, Don't save goes on and drops the changes, Cancel stays where you are. Return answers Save and Esc answers Cancel.
Save rack
Comes the first time a new rack is saved, and asks for its name. A name with nothing in it cannot be confirmed.
Save as
Asks for the name of the copy.
Save preset
Asks for the preset's name. The name is selected, so typing replaces it.
Update rack?
Comes after saving a preset in a rack that is already saved. Save rack saves the rack too; Not now leaves it unsaved. The preset is stored either way.
Remove channel?
Comes from the × on a channel. Remove takes the channel and its effects out of the rack, and that counts as a change like any other.

In a rack that was never saved, no question comes after saving a preset. A line says the preset is saved and adds It joins this rack when you save it.

A sound the app cannot read from a plugin keeps its last saved state, and the line says so: TB-303 could not be read, its last saved sound was kept.

Where racks are stored

Everything lives in ~/Library/Application Support/Rackmate:

configurations
The racks, one file each.
presets
Your presets, by plugin.
settings.json
What you set in Settings.

Rackmate used to be called Pluginarium. At launch, when the Rackmate folder does not exist and ~/Library/Application Support/Pluginarium does, the old folder is copied to the new one. This happens once. The old folder is never moved, changed or deleted, and what you save in Rackmate goes only to the new one. If the copy fails, the partial folder is removed and the app starts empty.

Settings

Open it from the Start screen, from the gear in the top bar, or with ⌘,. Done goes back. Settings belong to the app, not to a rack, and every change is stored at once. The page scrolls.

1Device
The audio device plugins play and listen through: System default or a device from the list.
2Sample rate
With a picked device: 44.1 kHz, 48 kHz or 96 kHz.
3Buffer size
With a picked device: 64 samples, 128 samples, 256 samples or 512 samples.
4Set by the system. Pick a device to change these.
Shown with System default, when the two fields above only report what runs.
5Clock source
None or a MIDI source. See MIDI clock.
6Instrument positions
See Position tables. Effect positions follow further down.

Audio device: System default or a picked device

System default
The app follows the default output of macOS and writes nothing to the device. It runs at the sample rate and buffer size the device already has, and the two fields show those values, dimmed. They cannot be changed here.
A picked device
The app writes the sample rate and buffer size to that device when audio starts and each time you change them. The stored defaults are 48 kHz and 128 samples, so picking a device can change the settings it had, for other apps too.

A new device or sample rate restarts audio and reloads the plugins; a new buffer size restarts the device under the same plugins. A picked device that is gone stays listed as (not connected). The list follows what macOS reports while Settings is on screen.

Position tables

A new channel takes its routing from its position in the rack: the first instrument takes row 1 of Instrument positions, the second takes row 2, and so on. Standalone effects count separately in Effect positions. Set the tables once for your studio, and change any channel afterwards if you need to. Changing a table does not change existing channels.

Instrument positions
Seven rows, each with MIDI device, Channel and Audio out. They start on No MIDI and Out 3–4, then Out 5–6, up to Out 15–16.
Effect positions
Three rows, each with Audio in and Audio out. They start on In 9–10 to Out 11–12, then one pair higher each row.

Beyond the last row, new channels reuse it. If the device does not have a position's channels, the new channel takes the first stereo pair no channel uses, or else the first pair of the device, so it plays at once instead of staying silent.

MIDI clock

Pick the source in Settings under Clock source. Plugins with tempo sync then follow it, with no setup on the channels.

Plugins never receive MIDI clock messages. The app reads the clock and gives each plugin the tempo, the position and whether the transport plays. Start, Stop and Continue from the source drive that transport, and Follow Start/Stop decides who sees it.

Hapax clock, 120.0 BPM
Clock is arriving. The lamp blinks on the beat.
No MIDI clock
No source is picked, or no clock has arrived for a second. It does not matter whether the source is still connected. Plugins keep the last tempo, or 120 BPM if there has been none. If the source goes silent while playing, the transport stops.

Faults

A rack always opens. What is missing shows where it belongs, as lit red text, and the rest plays.

What you seeWhat it meansWhat to do
1Hapax is not connected in red, in place of the device macOS no longer lists this MIDI source. Connect it, or click it and pick another source. The route comes back by itself when the source returns.
2Plugin name in red, and a Replace plugin key in place of the preset The plugin is not on this Mac. The tooltip of the name reads Plugin not installed on this Mac. Press Replace plugin and pick another, or install it and open the rack again. The channel's saved sound stays until you replace it.
3Red Audio out display and Out 5–6 is not available on this device The audio device lacks this output. An input it lacks lights its own display red. Pick another route on the channel, or use the device that has it.
4An effect with a red border, name and lamp That effect did not load or is not installed. The tooltip says which. Its name and lamp do not respond, it does not drag, and its only menu item is Remove. Remove it in its three-dot menu or install it. The channel's plugin plays on.
Plugin name in red, and the saved sound dimmed in place of the preset The plugin is there but would not start. The tooltip of the name begins Plugin failed to load and gives the reason. Open the rack again, or check the plugin in another host.
Loading, dimmed, in place of the preset The plugin is still starting. Some take a few seconds. Wait.
Audio device missing The device is gone. The rack stays open. Connect it. The rack plays again when the device returns. Or pick another device in Settings.
Audio could not start The device refused to start. The tooltip gives the reason. Check that the device works in other apps. The app tries again at each device change.
Waiting for microphone access macOS is asking you for the microphone. Nothing plays until you answer. Answer the macOS dialog. See Microphone permission.

Microphone permission

macOS treats every audio input as a microphone. The first time you open a rack on a device that has inputs, macOS asks whether Rackmate may use the microphone, with the reason Rackmate plays audio from your interface through your plugins. This happens even if no channel uses an input yet.

Until you answer, no audio device is open: nothing plays, and the top bar reads Waiting for microphone access. Settings, the menus and Quit keep working. Answer the macOS dialog, and the rack starts. macOS asks only once.

Allow
Audio in and Sidechain in work.
Don't Allow
Outputs still play, inputs are silent, and the top bar reads Microphone access denied, inputs are silent. Plugins that use no input are not affected.

To change the answer later, turn Rackmate on in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Microphone and start the app again.

Updates

At every launch Rackmate asks whether a newer version exists and, if so, offers it. Nothing is installed without your OK. Check for Updates… in the app menu asks right away. Without an internet connection the app starts as usual.

An installed Pluginarium 0.1.0 does not update to Rackmate. Install Rackmate from the download page; your racks come along.

Limits of this version

  • Audio Units only, of the types aumu, aumf and aufx. No VST or VST3, and no plugins that exist for Intel Macs only.
  • No MIDI learn, and no control of plugin parameters from MIDI controllers.
  • No faders, panning or level mixing. Your mixer does that.
  • No recording, sequencing or arranging.
  • One rack at a time. Loading another stops what plays, so it is not made for switching during a performance.
  • A sidechain comes from an input of the audio device, not from another channel.
  • No latency compensation. Audio that passes through the Mac arrives one round trip late, and a plugin played over MIDI sounds later than hardware triggered by the same sequencer. Keep the buffer low, and correct what remains on the sequencer.
  • No limiter. The app only silences invalid samples and clips what leaves a channel at +12 dB.
  • Channels cannot be reordered.
  • An effect that is turned off leaves the audio path, so its tail is cut.
  • Racks and presets cannot be renamed or deleted in the app. Save as gives a copy a new name. To remove a rack, delete its file in configurations while the app is closed. The name is inside the file, not in the file name.
  • The app does not start with the Mac. Launching it is up to you.
  • This interface has been tried on a MacBook with a four-output interface. Paths through a larger interface, a MIDI router, a hardware clock source and real sidechain plugins are still to be tried in a studio session.
